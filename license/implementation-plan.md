# Implementation Plan: 247

## Goal

Author SlopOn's complete licensing document set — a custom source-available "SlopOn Community License 1.0" (free for individuals in personal capacity and organizations under a $120k/year revenue-or-expenditure threshold, paid above it, redistribution prohibited for all tiers), a skeleton Commercial License Agreement, a Contributor Assignment Agreement, a plain-English licensing summary, and supporting files — delivered as Markdown documents in the task folder, ready to commit to the product repository.

## Acceptance criteria

- A canonical `LICENSE.md` exists in the task folder, titled **"SlopOn Community License 1.0"**, structured on the Business Source License 1.1 skeleton (parameters block → definitions → rights granted → restrictions → commercial-license requirement → term/termination → general clauses) with the change-date / open-source-conversion mechanism **removed**; the source remains proprietary indefinitely.
- The free tier is granted to exactly two classes: (a) individuals acting in **personal capacity** (which covers hobby/unpaid open-source contributions and solo freelance work); (b) organizations under the revenue/expenditure threshold. Charities and nonprofits are **not** separately exempt — every registered body is subject to the threshold. This is a deliberate refinement of the original task wording ("free for… charities, open source projects"): charities and open-source projects with revenue/expenditure under the threshold are free in practice, but no entity type receives an unconditional exemption. Work paid for or directed by an organization is that organization's use, even when the output is open source.
- The threshold is drafted exactly as agreed: an organization exceeds it when, aggregated across itself and all parents/subsidiaries/affiliates under common control, **either** gross revenue **or** total expenditure is (a) ≥ USD 120,000 over the trailing 12 months, **or** (b) ≥ USD 10,000 in any single month within the last 3 months (USD or foreign-currency equivalent), with a **90-day grace period** to purchase a commercial license after first crossing.
- Redistribution is prohibited for all tiers: no copying, distributing, sublicensing, selling, hosting, or publishing the software or derivative works as any product or service (competing or not); no removing or altering license/copyright notices; no circumventing license terms; a trademark clause reserves all rights to the "SlopOn" name and logo.
- Internal/private modification is expressly permitted (required for trust-building and contributions), but the only sanctioned way to share modifications is contributing them back to the official project under the Contributor Assignment Agreement.
- The license contains an explicit **"Output" clause** stating that code or other output generated by the Software using the user's own inputs is not a Derivative Work and is not subject to the license's restrictions; the Licensor claims no ownership of such output. This clause is mirrored in the Commercial License Agreement.
- A skeleton `COMMERCIAL-LICENSE.md` exists covering: parties, grant parameterized by an Order Form (license scope — seat count vs organization-wide — and fees are deferred to the order form; no pricing appears in the document), term and renewal, restrictions mirroring the community license, audit/verification right (with cost-allocation and non-compliance consequences), support and updates, warranty disclaimer, limitation of liability, IP indemnity, termination and effect, confidentiality, and general clauses with governing law **England and Wales**.
- A `CONTRIBUTOR-ASSIGNMENT-AGREEMENT.md` exists containing: full present-tense copyright assignment of contributions to the licensor, a moral-rights waiver to the fullest extent permitted, a fallback exclusive/irrevocable/perpetual/sublicensable license where assignment is legally ineffective, a patent grant, a non-exclusive license-back to the contributor for their own contribution, contribution-origin representations (original work, authority, employer consent where applicable, disclosure of third-party material), and governing law England and Wales. A short `CONTRIBUTING.md` enforces it ("by submitting a contribution you agree to the CAA").
- A short **plain-English licensing summary** (`LICENSING.md`) of no more than 15 lines exists, clearly marked as non-binding, covering: who can use SlopOn free, when a paid license is required, what is prohibited, and where to get a commercial license.
- All documents carry the licensor placeholder **"[Full Legal Name], trading as SlopOn (https://slopon.dev)"** and a `PRE-LAUNCH-CHECKLIST.md` lists every placeholder to fill (licensor legal name, commercial contact email, order-form/pricing URL) plus a mandatory **independent review by an England & Wales solicitor** before launch.
- The documents are mutually consistent: capitalized terms carry identical meanings across documents, all cross-references resolve, every bracketed placeholder is inventoried in the pre-launch checklist, and all Markdown renders cleanly (GitHub preview).

## Assumptions

- No legal entity exists yet, so the licensor is written as the placeholder "[Full Legal Name], trading as SlopOn (https://slopon.dev)" — a domain is not a legal person and cannot be the licensor; a natural person's name carries the legal weight pre-incorporation. Rationale: user's chosen option.
- Governing law and venue are **England and Wales**. Rationale: explicit user decision.
- Pricing and the commercial license's scope model (per-seat vs organization-wide) are deferred to an Order Form referenced by the commercial agreement skeleton. Rationale: user requested a skeleton agreement only; the Order Form is the standard mechanism for deferring commercial variables without blocking the legal text.
- Enforcement is contractual, not technical: no license keys, activation, or telemetry are in scope. Rationale: task is limited to authoring the license; technical enforcement is a separate engineering task.
- There is no standalone "open source project" or "charity" exemption clause. Charities and open-source projects benefit from the threshold like any other organization: those under the threshold are free in practice; those above it must purchase a license. This is a deliberate refinement of the original task description's wording ("free for… charities, open source projects"), confirmed by the user. Employer-paid open-source work is organizational use subject to the threshold. Rationale: user confirmed the threshold-only approach after being presented with the corporate-OSS and large-charity scenarios.
- The product repository is not available in this workspace, so all deliverables are authored in `/task-247/` and will be committed to the product repo later. Rationale: user chose "task folder only" for deliverables; the website repo is intentionally untouched.
- Documents are authored in English only; translations are out of scope.
- These documents are not legal advice; they follow the structure of the lawyer-reviewed BSL 1.1 but are custom text. Rationale: mitigated by the mandatory pre-launch solicitor review.

## Risks and mitigations

- The license is custom text, not lawyer-vetted boilerplate: mitigated by modeling the structure on the lawyer-reviewed BSL 1.1 and by the mandatory pre-launch review by an England & Wales solicitor recorded in `PRE-LAUNCH-CHECKLIST.md`.
- Copyright assignment in the CAA is unenforceable in some jurisdictions (e.g. Germany, where authors cannot fully assign copyright): mitigated by the fallback exclusive/irrevocable/sublicensable license clause and the moral-rights waiver to the extent permitted.
- Threshold compliance is self-reported and enforcement is honor-system until technical enforcement exists: mitigated by the audit/verification clause in the commercial agreement (records, annual certification, audit right with cost-shifting on non-compliance) and a noted follow-up engineering task for license keys.
- Organizations may attempt to evade the threshold via shell entities, splitting revenue, or engaging under-threshold contractors: mitigated by the Affiliates/common-Control aggregation clause and the contractor attribution rule (work performed for an Organization's benefit is that Organization's use regardless of employment status).
- Applying the threshold to charities and nonprofits (rather than granting an unconditional exemption) may attract public criticism at launch: accepted by the task author as a deliberate decision; mitigated by preparing positioning/FAQ messaging at launch time (outside this task's scope).
- A placeholder (licensor name, contact email, pricing URL, liability cap, audit parameters) could accidentally ship: mitigated by the Step 5 placeholder sweep and the pre-launch checklist, which makes placeholder replacement an explicit gated step.
- Future edits to the license could create ambiguity about which version governs a given copy: mitigated by the version identifier ("1.0") and the version-policy clause in the General section.
- Charities' status differs by jurisdiction (not all are "registered bodies"): mitigated by drafting the Organization definition to cover any legal person or entity other than a natural person in Personal Capacity, including unincorporated bodies, rather than enumerating entity types.
- Without an explicit Output clause, a court could interpret SlopOn-generated code as a Derivative Work, triggering the distribution ban on users' own generated code: mitigated by the dedicated Output clause (§3) in both the Community and Commercial licenses, which explicitly states Output is not a Derivative Work and is not encumbered.
- The total distribution ban may face enforceability challenges in EU jurisdictions with strong exhaustion doctrines: to be assessed during the mandatory solicitor review; the solicitor may recommend jurisdiction-specific savings language.
